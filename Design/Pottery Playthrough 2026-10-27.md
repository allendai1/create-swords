## Progression speed

- **First tier, $100–$180:** one or two sales each. Buys came at 5:27, 6:52 and 7:56, about one a minute once selling started.
- **Second tier, $350–$450:** 2.9–3.75× the first tier, about 3–5 sales each at roughly $90 a sale. A buy every 3–4 minutes is likely next, but the video ends before that.
- **Offer size varies a lot:** +10% to +139% on similar bowls ($63 vs $143). Each sale is a small gamble, which gives a reason to wait for an offer rather than auto-sell.
- **The cycle:** clay → wheel (~5–10 s) → glaze (~6–12 s) → kiln (15 s) → shelf takes about 40–60 s with walking. Active minigame time is about 16 s of that. The rest is walking and the kiln.
- **Unbought at the end:** Cup Shape $180 (the second recipe), Parking Space $250, Fire Speed $450, New Space $350, Shelf 3 $350. A locked "Auto Roll" button sits on the clay lever.

## Lessons for Sell Swords!

1. **First sale under 2 minutes, but protect the second.** Pottery's first sale lands at 1:44. Then a second tutorial and no-offer walk-ins push the next coin to 5:21, which is the slump Allen felt. The second sale should come fast: no tutorial between sale 1 and sale 2, and the first post-tutorial walk-in always makes an offer.
2. **Sped-up first wait, honest later waits.** A 2× kiln in the tutorial and real 15 s afterwards worked. A first upgrade that only shaves about 1 s (Fire Speed Lv 1) is a weak first buy. Our first speed upgrade should feel different.
3. **Offers that expire at the shop create the tension.** A 60 s timer that runs while you're away, plus a single kiln, makes "make the next one or go check" a real choice. Compare with our cashier and walk-in offers in `ShopService`.
4. **First tier ≈ 1–2 sales, second tier ≈ 3×.** Prices sit just out of reach on the walking path, and every buy shows the next price right away.
5. **Requests teach a second currency without walking.** The phone sends the order remotely and pays gems. The tutorial then aims at an upgrade the player can't afford yet, and Daily Rewards (day 7 = 60) closes the gap.

## Compared with Sell Swords!

Our numbers come from `Development/economy_model.py`, run on 2026-10-27 with the live `Shared/Upgrades` prices (Economy pass 2), the live `ShopService` offer timer (45 s) and walk-in gaps (12–22 s until Ore Slot 2, then 45–75 s), for its three players. The model times swords, not reading prompts or walking between them, so **these are estimates, not playtest measurements**. The model's first sword is worth 42 (a Common tantō). Since pass 2 the tutorial's second pull is an Uncommon, so the live first sale is about 64. The script is in the session scratchpad, not the vault.

|---|---|---|

| Second sale | 5:21, **3:37 later** | **3:07, 1:36 later** (2:00 / 4:32) |

What it says:
1. **First sale and second-sale gap: ours is already ahead.** Same first sale (about 64, about 1:30), and our second comes about 2 minutes sooner. The 12–22 s tutorial walk-ins and offer-every-time customers prevent the slump Allen felt. Keep both until Ore Slot 2.
2. **Our early buys are cheaper and more frequent.** Pottery makes every buy cost 1.5–2 sales and empty the wallet, then shows a price about 3× higher. Ours are pocket change until the Polishing Station. That suits a guided tutorial; after "Workshop open!" the 210–520 pads still cost only 1–3 sales each.
3. **Our felt jump comes late.** Pottery's ×3 hits at the second level of every item (minute 6–8). Ours is one cliff at the Katana (2000). Pottery's pattern would mean repricing pads in pairs (level 1 cheap, level 2 ×3). That is not needed while the 20-minute Katana is the goal.
4. **Our offers are shorter and safer.** 45 s against 60, and no gamble in the amount. Pottery's +10%–139% spread and customers who don't offer make each walk to the shop a bet. This is the "VIP buyers with tastes" idea already in [[Design/Progression and Economy Review — 2026-10-27]] §5.
5. **Not measured:** none of our numbers are timed in play. Time the first two sales and the first five buys in the next fresh-start playtest, then put the real times in this table.
