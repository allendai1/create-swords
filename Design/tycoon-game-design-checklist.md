# Tycoon Game Design Checklist

Each section targets one feeling that makes tycoon games hard to put down: growth, anticipation, pride, efficiency, urgency, flow and long-term goals. Tick items off as you build them in.

## Growth and progress

Goal: effort turns into visible results fast.

- [ ] Core loop is clear within the first minute: earn, buy, earn faster
- [ ] First upgrades are cheap and fast, so new players get several wins right away
- [ ] Every purchase visibly changes the base (new machine, new room, more customers)
- [ ] Income clearly speeds up after upgrades, in numbers and effects the player can feel

## Anticipation

Goal: the player always has something they're about to get.

- [ ] The next purchase is always visible with its price
- [ ] A progress bar or cash counter shows how close the player is
- [ ] There's always one short-term goal (next upgrade) and one long-term goal (unlock a new area)

## Ownership and pride

Goal: the base feels like the player's own creation.

- [ ] Each player gets their own plot
- [ ] Some choice in layout, decor or upgrade order
- [ ] Other players can see and visit your base

## Efficiency and optimization

Goal: wasted time or resources feel wrong, and playing smart feels rewarding.

- [ ] Idle resources feel wasteful (empty shelves, a full storage bin, cash waiting to be collected)
- [ ] Smart choices are rewarded: better upgrade order, faster restock routes, combo bonuses
- [ ] Stats the player can improve, like income per minute or sales per hour

## Urgency and loss aversion

Goal: there's something the player could miss, so they stay alert.

- [ ] Some opportunities are timed: offers, rush customers, special orders
- [ ] Missing one costs a small amount, never progress the player already has
- [ ] Clear warnings before something expires (sound, flashing, countdown)

## Plate-spinning and flow

Goal: juggling tasks keeps the player focused and losing track of time.

- [ ] At least two tasks compete for attention (make vs. sell)
- [ ] Task pacing ramps up as the player upgrades
- [ ] Short travel between stations, so juggling feels snappy rather than tedious

## Feeling needed, then freedom

Goal: the player's presence matters early, and automation is earned later.

- [ ] Early game: active play clearly beats being idle
- [ ] Mid-game: automation upgrades (helpers, auto-sell) gradually reward the player with freedom
- [ ] Offline or idle income, so leaving doesn't feel like failing

## Social

Goal: seeing other players motivates you to keep going.

- [ ] Leaderboard or visible status for rich players
- [ ] Neighbors' bases sit within view to spark comparison

## Long-term replay

Goal: there's always a reason to start another run.

- [ ] Rebirth/prestige resets progress for a permanent multiplier
- [ ] New unlocks after rebirth, so the second run feels different

## Health check

Goal: the game is compelling without feeling punishing.

- [ ] Losses sting but never wipe out progress
- [ ] Players can reach a natural stopping point, such as finishing an order or filling the shelves
- [ ] Playtest: would someone *want* to come back, not just feel they *have* to?
