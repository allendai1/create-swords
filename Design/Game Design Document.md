# Sell Swords! — Game Design Document

*Working title: **Sell Swords!** (alternatives were Create Swords! and Craft Swords!).*

> Refined from the original brainstorm in [[Archive/Create Swords!|Create Swords!]]. The brainstorm is left untouched; this note is the working design.
> Direction approved 2026-10-19, with later dated decisions incorporated through 2026-10-24. The latest dated decision takes precedence over older rules. This document describes the intended game, not a claim that the changes are implemented. Earlier prototype details remain in [[Development/Code Map|Code Map]], session notes, and [[Archive/Design/GDD before 2026-10-19 revision|GDD before 2026-10-19 revision]]. New onboarding proposals are separate in [[Archive/Design/Core Loop Review|Core Loop Review]].

---

## 1. One-line pitch

**Build your smithy in a Japanese mountain village, forge swords by hand, and earn a reputation among sword wielders from across the world.**

A cozy crafting tycoon inspired by the atmosphere and craftsmanship of Demon Slayer's Swordsmith Village. Daily village life is peaceful, punctuated by spectacular individual visitors. Original locals share the world with recognizable fictional and historical sword users, including knights and other non-Japanese traditions. Their coexistence needs no crossover explanation. Village-wide events are deferred.

## 2. Pillars

**Progression direction confirmed 2026-10-22:** relaxed swordmaking serves the larger fantasy of becoming rich. Make more valuable swords by hand, invest profits in familiar production, then personally pursue the next designs and special commissions. Most upgrades and blueprints are money-led; milestone commissions introduce special designs and sword traditions. After personally crafting a sword shape, pay coins to train employees to produce it in any permitted material supplied to them; no separate training per material combination. Fully trained smiths can match the player's 100% forging quality. Initially the player supplies rolled ore; a later ore attendant runs the existing roller using the same material odds. Training costs and ownership remain open; see [[Design/Workshop Growth#Wealth and staged automation (confirmed 2026-10-22)]].

1. **Making a sword feels satisfying.** Every hammer hit visibly shapes the blade, with sparks, sound, and movement.
2. **Simple actions, optional extra care.** Hitting every forging circle gives 100% quality; optional final polishing on the assembled sword adds 10% to the sword's whole value (Allen 2026-10-20; on the whole value since 2026-10-23). Quality remains a visible percentage and affects price. Employee output has separate capability limits (see §10.4).
3. **Every customer is a little story.** Flexible everyday tastes, personal relationships, and specific special commissions give swords a purpose.
4. **A workshop worth growing.** Sales fund functional upgrades; crafting milestones unlock fittings; commissions introduce sword traditions.
5. **A cozy home for extraordinary visitors.** Japanese village identity, varied customers, no combat or testing-area scope for now.

## 3. Target & platform

- **Platform:** Roblox (PC, mobile, console). All interactions must work with touch: the anvil minigame is tap/click based.
- **Audience:** 9–16, fans of *Create Pottery!*, cozy tycoons and simulator games.
- **Session:** 10–30 minutes, with clear crafting and progression goals. No quench/temper step.

## 4. Core loop

```text
Roll ingot → Heat → Hit every forging circle (100%) → Seat the hilt on the anvil → (Polish, +10% value) → (Etch, unlocked station)
    ↑                                                                     ↓
Upgrades ← Coins + reputation ← Display and haggle / fulfill an accepted order

Direct commissions, optional courier deliveries, and relationships provide goals alongside ordinary shop sales.
```

| Step | Action | Current direction |
|---|---|---|
| Roll | Ore loot box (Ore Roller) | Since 2026-10-22, a loot box in the workshop's back-left corner, from Allen's Call of Duty loot box idea ([[Art/Ore Loot Box]]). Before it there were placeholders: the supply chest (2026-10-21) and the alcove; the 2026-10-20 mine and carts are parked ([[Archive/Art/Blender Station Review]]). Pull the shared lever: the lid opens, one ingot per bought slot (up to 6) floats up, spins through the ore types and locks in on its result, and all drop onto the lid, in 3.6 s. **Spin as often as you like** (Allen 2026-10-24: "Players should be able to spin the ores as many times as they want"): the lever is off only while a roll plays, and pulling it with ore still waiting replaces that ore (Economy pass 2, 2026-10-27, Allen: "just spin new ones, existing ones are replaced"; from 2026-10-24 it went into your hands first, all of it, as there is no limit on ingots in hand, [[Sessions/2026-10-24#No hand limit, 1.7× ingots, labels that don't overlap]]); anything you carry can be dropped on the bag's trash. This supersedes "collect before rolling again" (2026-10-22), which had replaced the unlimited re-rolls of 2026-10-20. With Ore Slots 2–6 bought, **Merge Ore Slots** trades them for more luck (§10.2). Keep the full fantasy material roster. |
| Heat | Forge | Load an ingot and collect it after heating. One furnace heats **one ingot at a time** (Allen 2026-10-20; the forge model shows one tray), and its label shows only the time left, then "Ready!"; heating more at once means buying another furnace in the tycoon. Base heating time is **10 seconds** (approved 2026-10-19, built 2026-10-22), replacing the 15-second prototype duration. **Until the tutorial's letter is sent an ingot heats in 3 seconds** (Allen 2026-10-22: "first time tutorial, the furnace and other processes need to be sped up"; until 2026-10-31 it was only until the first sword was finished). No quench or cooling countdown while shaping. |
| Forge | Anvil | Choose a hot ingot and unlocked blueprint. Tap anywhere (or Space) to strike, without timing windows or misses (no strike circle since 2026-11-10). Each hit improves the blade; completing every strike gives **100% quality** (Allen 2026-10-20; was 90%). The anvil view closes when the blade is done. |
| Assemble | Anvil (since 2026-10-28; was the Hilt Bench) | Right after the blade is finished (all strikes, or Finish early); its hilt is fitted automatically (since 2026-10-23: the blueprint's own hilt shape, in the best tier unlocked). Attach it by dragging the hilt along onto the tang in one motion (or holding Space / A); it seats with a clang (Allen 2026-10-22, replacing the three knocks of 2026-10-20). Since 2026-10-30 the seat hits harder the rarer the metal, and the finished sword lifts, faces you and gleams before it drops into the inventory (a press skips it). No timing bonus or penalty. |
| Polish, optionally | Polishing Station | On the finished sword: scrubbing anywhere on the blade with a round brush wipes off a grime layer (since 2026-11-03, [[Sessions/2026-11-03#Polishing by scrubbing]]; before, dragging along the edge); each section counts as polished when its whole strip is clean, and the rest snaps clean at 95%. The brush is where the cursor or finger is (on a phone it sat a little above the finger until 2026-11-07, [[Sessions/2026-11-07#Polishing on a phone: the brush is under the finger]]). A spot takes about three passes of the brush (two until 2026-11-07), and half a second after the blade is fully polished a white streak runs across the blade from guard to tip, speeding up as it goes (half a second), with a sword-draw ring (2026-11-07, [[Sessions/2026-11-07#Polishing takes more passes and ends in a glint]]). The view centres the sword, hilt to tip, on the stone, and while you polish the stone turns dark slate under a light blade (a dark metal keeps the pale stone), with a key light (cool white) and a little more contrast, and the clean blade shows a bright reflection band that comes up where you scrub (2026-11-09, [[Sessions/2026-11-09#Crafter cards show the whole sword; tutorial line text solid; polish view centred and darker]]). A fully polished sword is worth **×1.10** (its whole value since 2026-10-23; added to the other bonuses before); it was named "(Polished)" until 2026-10-24, when the tag left every sword name; quality is already 100% from forging (Allen 2026-10-20). The anvil only forges. |
| Etch, optionally (last step) | Etching Table | Apply a finish pattern. The last step, after polishing, because the Etching Table will be an unlocked station (Allen 2026-10-20). Current approved interim flow is free and instant; a trace minigame is deferred. |
| Display | Sword holders | Put the complete sword on display. Stock influences regular visitors. Players start with one 3-sword wall rack in the Starter Shop, then buy a second wall rack, a display table, the Upgraded Store and more racks and tables from tycoon floor pads ([[Design/Tycoon Upgrades]], 2026-10-20). Since 2026-10-22 every wall rack is a three-tier sword shelf (concept art `sword shelf.png`) and every display table is Allen's imported SwordTable with three stands. A displayed sword is centred on its holder, which closes in under a short one. Swords on display wear a thin silver line on the edge that follows your viewing angle; polished ones pulse along it (shop shine, 2026-11-03). |
| Sell | Customer | Accept or haggle; pushing for more can cause the customer to leave. Sales earn coins and build reputation and familiarity. |
| Deliver, optionally | Delivery board and courier/cart | Fulfill a separate remote order, pack swords (chosen on a packing page since 2026-10-24, §9.1), dispatch, and collect the reward when the courier returns. Direct commissions retain their immediate picker hand-in. |
| Grow | Workshop and commissions | Upgrade the workshop, unlock fittings through crafting, and learn new sword traditions through commissions. |

**Picker behavior (confirmed 2026-10-19):** every item-selection interaction opens its picker every time, even with only one valid item. Do not auto-select the current workpiece or bypass a picker. **One exception since 2026-10-24:** holding a cold ingot and pressing E at the furnace puts that ingot in, with no picker; the picker opens only with empty hands (or a hot ingot or a sword). Closing the anvil's picker with no blade on the anvil now leaves the anvil view. Commission delivery is a parallel route to ordinary display sales: open the commission, select the finished sword through a picker, and receive the agreed reward. No display listing or haggling is required for commission delivery.

## 5. Forging and optional grinding

**Current station split:** the anvil forges; the Polishing Station performs the former freehand grinding as an optional step after hilt assembly; etching comes after it, last (2026-10-20). The section title is retained for existing links. Player forging completes at 100% quality; optional polish is a separate +10% finish bonus (2026-10-20). Older employee quality/recovery rules in §10.4 need reconciliation with this change.

**Approved 2026-10-19; replaces the timed Points and freehand hammering prototypes as the intended main mode.**

- **Forging camera (approved 2026-10-19):** slightly angled overhead, showing the blade outline clearly while retaining visible thickness, hammer movement, and the anvil. Keep the entire blade visible throughout lengthening and repositioning. Exact framing and camera movement remain to be tuned. Since 2026-11-10 (Allen, on a phone) the view is closer (0.8 of the closest distance, backing off only for a blade that wouldn't fit) and the anvil sits lower on the screen. [[Sessions/2026-11-10#Anvil: tap anywhere, camera lower and closer]]
- **Universal starting blank (approved 2026-10-19):** every sword blueprint starts from the same blank shape and size. Forging stretches it to the selected blade’s length and establishes its distinct profile; do not use blueprint-sized starting blanks. The rolled material still determines its material appearance. **Since 2026-11-08 the blank is the ingot you carried, in its shape, sized to fit the anvil face** (38.1 × 19.9 × 12.4 cm, 1.91 × 0.99 × 0.62 studs; since 2026-11-09 the whole anvil show is drawn at 0.7 of that, 1.34 × 0.70 × 0.44 studs, the sword at true size again at hilting; the ingot model lies centred on the anvil until the first blow slides it under the hammer). The lengthen stage takes off about half its extra bulk, the taper most of the rest and the point the last, so every blow takes metal off; short blades get shorter ([[Sessions/2026-11-08#The anvil starts from the real ingot]]).
- Guided circles identify hammer strikes. ~~**The blade moves by itself, without visible tongs:** it slides, turns, and flips beneath the fixed hammer point between strikes.~~ **Since 2026-11-11 the blade lies still, centred on the anvil, and the hammer comes to each strike's spot** (Allen, after comparing the Forge Lab's versions: "just keep the enhanced original view with no arms"). Players do not manually reposition it.
- **Forging feel (2026-11-11, chosen in the Forge Lab):** each blow winds up, whips down, holds on the metal for 70 ms and recoils. The metal gives at once under the hammer and the rest of the blade follows over 0.12 s. The hot blade is drawn yellow-orange at the core, darker at the edges, cooling a little with every blow, with the struck spot flaring white. Sparks stay at the contact, with a flat shockwave, a low thud under the clang and a small downward camera kick. The last blow winds higher, lands the whole remaining shape at once with a wider burst and a bigger kick (stronger for Epic and rarer metals), and the save starts straight away. The sword's blade arrives orange on the hilt seat and cools to steel with a puff of steam while play goes on. The camera glides in and out of the view. Long blades are drawn smaller so the finished blade is at most 1.15 anvil faces long. Tuned in `StarterPlayerScripts.AnvilFeel`. [[Sessions/2026-11-11#Forge Lab shipped: Enhanced, camera A, no arm]]
- Players tap anywhere to strike (2026-11-10, was a circle to hit; [[Sessions/2026-11-10#Anvil: tap anywhere, camera lower and closer]]). No overall timer, timing window, misses, cooling countdown, or reheat loop during shaping. Earlier retry/minimum-hit discussions are superseded by this simpler rule.
- During the tutorial the anvil shows "Tap to shape" until Finish opens and a ghost hand pressing on the blade until the first strike (2026-11-11, [[Sessions/2026-11-11#Anvil: tutorial ghost hand and "Tap to shape"]]).
- While the blade is hot it wears a thin cream outline, which fades as it cools, so the blade stands out from the anvil (2026-11-07, trial). [[Sessions/2026-11-07#Hot blade outline on the anvil]]
- **Staged shaping (approved 2026-10-19):** early hits lengthen the thick blank; middle hits establish the blade’s taper and curve where appropriate; final hits form the point and refine the rough outline. Each hammer impact visibly changes the section beneath it. The blade progresses from a thick blank to a long rough blade to a recognizable sword. Exact hit counts, intermediate shapes, and animation pacing remain to be designed.
- **Impact deformation (approved 2026-10-19):** quickly and smoothly deform the struck area at hammer contact, blending the change into neighboring metal. Flatten or stretch the local section as appropriate to the shaping stage. Avoid discrete shape snaps, wobbling, or a slow melting effect. Exact deformation timing and falloff remain to be tuned.
- Each completed circle shapes its section and increases quality. All circles completed gives **100% player quality** (revised 2026-10-20). **Initial implementation uses the same circle/hit count across sword blueprints. Higher-tier swords should eventually require modestly more effort**, rather than making all larger swords automatically take longer. Exact tier thresholds, added effort, circle count, and transition pacing remain to be tuned; the previous 12 is only a prototype baseline. No timing challenge or failure penalty is introduced by this direction.
- **Forged blade appearance (original art direction, 2026-10-19):** clean and recognizable, with the intended silhouette, subtle excess metal along the edge, and a dull finish. It should already look like a desirable finished sword after fitting the hilt, not a visibly jagged or misshapen failure. Optional polishing refines the small edge allowance and improves the finish; it adds a finish bonus rather than completing missing player forging quality.
- Forging ends at **100%** (changed 2026-10-20 from 90%) and the blade is finished right away; its hilt is seated on the anvil at once (2026-10-28; before, it went to the Hilt Bench). (Changed 2026-10-20: grinding moved from the anvil to the Polishing Station, an optional step on the finished sword.)
- **Faster Strikes I–V (2026-10-30)** speed up the strike cycle (0.75 s → ~0.375 s at V); the 12 strikes and their stages don't change ([[Sessions/2026-10-30#Faster Strikes]]).
- **Sparks build with each strike (2026-11-08):** 10 on the first blow up to 46 on the 10th, doubled on the finishing blow, plus more with each Faster Strikes level ([[Sessions/2026-11-08#More anvil sparks]]). Since 2026-11-10 they are our own art: hot streaks that cool from white-gold to red, a ray flare at the contact, a few embers and an orange light pulse ([[Sessions/2026-11-10#Anvil sparks: our own art]]).
- **10 strikes since 2026-10-30** (were 12; Allen: "Yes do 10 hits"): lengthen 3, taper 3, the point 4; the finished blade is the same; Finish opens at 8 of 10 (80%, ×1.6) ([[Sessions/2026-10-30#The anvil takes 10 hits (subagent)]]).
- Polishing (at the Polishing Station, on the assembled sword) is scrubbing a grime layer off the blade (since 2026-11-03), still constrained to remove only excess metal toward the target. It cannot overcut or reduce quality. A full polish multiplies the whole value by **1.10** (since 2026-10-23; before, a +10% finish bonus added to the hilt and etch bonuses). Scrubbing sounds like a stone rub, low and gritty on grime and rising as the spot comes clean (2026-11-09). A polished sword keeps the reflection band everywhere, in your hand, on racks and on display, fainter at half polish (2026-11-09); your own body is hidden while you polish. The polishing action is free after unlocking the station; the station costs 30 coins since 2026-10-30 (200 before; [[Design/Tycoon Upgrades]]). Selling unpolished stays a choice.
- Quality is shown as a percentage, not a grade name. It is now a guided completion/refinement measure; the old silhouette mismatch formula is not the new scoring specification.
- **How numbers read (2026-11-07):** counts put × first or between ("×2", "3× Katana"); multipliers put a plain x after ("2x Customers", "1.2x"); percentages are for quality, chances, progress and small bonuses ("92%", "+15% value"). One unit per stat everywhere it shows ([[Sessions/2026-11-07#× and % audit]]).
- Accent lines and bevels remain automatic visual details on finished swords, suited to the blueprint.
- Server validation must enforce valid circle progress and bounded grinding improvements. The recorded BladeCore/ForgeCheck integration covers this flow; see [[Development/Code Map]] and [[Current Status]] for implementation and remaining validation.

**Balance still to test:** whether polishing feels satisfying and genuinely optional for its +10% value bonus, how its progress reads, and how many forging circles keep repeated crafting enjoyable.

## 6. Materials, blueprints & finishes

### 6.1 Materials (rolled at the Ore Roller)

**Roller behavior (revised 2026-10-22, 2026-10-24):** one shared lever rolls every bought slot (up to six) in a 3.6-second loot-box show. Each slot holds one result on the lid. **Since 2026-10-24 you can roll as often as you like** (supersedes "collect all waiting results before rolling again", 2026-10-22): the lever is off only while a roll plays, and a pull replaces the results still waiting (since Economy pass 2, 2026-10-27; from 2026-10-24 it put them into your hands; a NEW card still comes when you collect a first-time metal). The tutorial's first roll teaches the pick: a Common, the guide's "Pull again for a rarer metal", then an Uncommon. Collection does not start another roll. Buying another ore slot increases results per pull without changing per-result rarity odds; merging the slots raises luck (§10.2). The 2026-10-22 rule superseded the older one that skipped occupied stands while rolling empty ones. See [[Art/Ore Loot Box]]. The roll ends with a chime when the last ingot lands, only if one of them is a metal you've never rolled (Allen's recording, since 2026-11-10; new-only the same day).

### 6.2 Blueprints (sword shapes)

**Blade art direction (approved 2026-10-19):** grounded starter tantō, wakizashi, and katana, with slightly exaggerated proportions for clear readability. Give each its own silhouette rather than simply scaling one katana. Later special blueprints can introduce extravagant fantasy shapes and details. Exact profiles and forging transformation choreography remain under design. Since 2026-11-10 a blade can have one see-through hole (a slot, a moon), punched through as the last forging stage reaches it; etches stop at its rim [[Sessions/2026-11-10#Blades can have holes]]. A hole may also open out of the blade's side as a fork at the tip (S05's two prongs) [[Sessions/2026-11-10#S05 fork]]. The 22 new silhouette swords (2026-11-10) are forgeable from the Dev Panel only (learned there, worth 1, no pad, not in the Book) until Allen picks which go in [[Sessions/2026-11-10#New silhouettes as real swords (in progress)]]. Inversion (2026-11-11, a tall prong and a short one with a V between them) is modelled and in the Studio gallery but not in the game until Allen releases it [[Sessions/2026-11-11#Inversion: the twin-prong sword (Studio only)]].

**Japanese starting progression: tantō → wakizashi → katana.** Players start with the **tantō blueprint** and simple on-screen instructions for their first sword. Later local teaching commissions introduce **wakizashi, then katana**, providing each blueprint when its commission begins. **Superseded 2026-10-23 (Allen: "the different swords you get are earned by just buying them through the tycoon pads"):** the Wakizashi Blueprint (60 coins) and Katana Blueprint (400, after it) are bought on one pad spot by the anvil; buying one learns it. Saves that learned a blueprint from a commission count its pad as bought.

Later visitors introduce other sword traditions through a first commission that supplies its required pattern. Additional blueprints within that tradition can be purchased with coins. Longswords, cutlasses, rapiers, claymores, and flamberges remain expansion candidates; their order and prices are not yet tuned. **Built 2026-10-23 (Allen: "Lets do 3 western swords, the longsword, falchion, and the gladius"):** the three are bought on the same blueprint pad after the Katana Sword (Longsword 1,000 → Falchion 2,000 → Gladius 3,500; placeholder prices), not through a commission. Shapes: [[Development/Blade Profile Authoring#Western blades (2026-10-23)]]; other candidates: [[Design/Sword Profile Ideas]].

**Big swords (proposed 2026-10-23, Allen: "a new category of swords that are big swords... ones that take two hands to hold or are different than the typical silhoute. the big swords would require you to purchase a standing big sword rack and it only holds one. they can sell for a lot"):** oversized two-handed blueprints, each shown only on a bought standing rack that holds one, priced well above ordinary swords. ### 6.4 Sword value formula

```
Value = Blueprint base
      × Material multiplier
      × Quality multiplier (×1.0 at 80% → ×2.0 at 100%)
      × (1 + hilt bonus + seat bonus + etch bonus)
      × 1.10 if fully polished (since 2026-10-23)
      × NPC preference bonus (see §7)
```

The tooltip on a finished sword shows each factor so players learn what to chase (not built; since 2026-10-22 a card after hilt, polish and etch shows the sword's value and that step's gain). Since 2026-10-20 every player-forged blade is 100% (×2.0); since 2026-10-23 a full polish multiplies the whole value by 1.10 (it was a +10% finish bonus added to the others). The seat bonus is always the full +6% since the drag seating. Review that incentive in [[Archive/Design/Core Loop Review|Core Loop Review]]. Employee quality below 90% needs explicit value/refinement rules before implementation, particularly if starting quality is below the table's 80% baseline; do not invent extrapolation. Optional finishes remain optional; other proposed bonuses still need tuning.

**The anvil's shape chip (since 2026-10-31):** it shows what that sword sells for forged with every strike, with its hilt and the seat bonus, unpolished; the polishing station's "now" value then matches it. It used to show only base × metal (Allen: "at the forge it says 720 coins ... once im at the polishing station, the price is increased to 1018"). **Since 2026-11-08 the chip is the price alone** (the difficulty stars are gone: forging doesn't use them; Allen: "the stars don't really mean much"), and the card shows the whole sword, polished, with the hilt the anvil will fit, on its metal's rarity colour like a Hotbar slot ([[Sessions/2026-11-08#Anvil cards show the whole sword]], [[Sessions/2026-11-08#Picker cards look like the Hotbar's slots]]). The crafter's Swords picker shows the same whole sword with the hilt crafters fit (2026-11-09). Since 2026-11-11 each crafter makes their own pick (one on Random doesn't change the others; [[Sessions/2026-11-11#Each crafter makes their own sword]]).

### 6.5 Hilt Bench and fittings

- **Since 2026-10-28 the hilt goes on at the anvil** (Allen: "Fold it in"; an early Finish also goes to the hilting): the server fits it when the blade is saved and the anvil plays the drag-on seat. The Hilt Bench was then removed, and carried bare blades with it ([[Sessions/2026-10-28#Hilt Bench removed, carried blades gone]]). [[Sessions/2026-10-28#Hilt Bench folded into the anvil]]

- Choose a blade and fittings, then attach by **dragging the hilt onto the tang** (one motion, untimed; 2026-10-22 replaced the three taps). No timing challenge or quality penalty.
- Fittings **match the sword tradition**: Japanese blades use appropriate wrapped grips and tsuba; western swords use their own guards and pommels. No cross-tradition mixing.
- Fittings unlock through **swords-crafted milestones**, without a later coin-purchase requirement.
- Exact fitting sets, milestone counts, and price bonuses need tuning. The old universal Wooden Grip → Gem Pommel ladder is superseded.
- **Built 2026-10-22:** every blueprint and hilt has a tradition, and the bench (and the server) only fit hilts of the blade's tradition. All six current hilts are Japanese koshirae; their names still come from the old ladder and the milestones are unchanged (0/2/4/7/10/15 swords).
- **Superseded 2026-10-23: nothing is chosen** (Allen: "the correct hilt is automatically applied.. so japanese swords have a japanese hilt, and western swords have their respective hilts. no need to choose"). Each blueprint names its hilt shape: the katana hilt for the tantō, wakizashi and katana, and its own hilt for the longsword, falchion and gladius. The six tiers became tradition-free colourings of that shape, and the bench and the crafter fit the best one unlocked. Picking a blade at the bench goes straight to seating. See [[Sessions/2026-10-23#Western swords and automatic hilts]].
- Completed swords can be held, collected, displayed, or sold. Clicking with a held sword swings a four-slash combo, one slash per click (2026-10-23, looks only: nothing is hit). Combat and a testing area are outside current scope.

## 8. Selling & haggling

**Tutorial exception (approved 2026-10-19):** the first customer gives a guaranteed fair offer, providing a clear reward for the first sword. Haggling is introduced on the **second ordinary shop sale**, not the first. Commission deliveries pay their agreed reward and do not count as shop sales for this tutorial step. The exact fair-price calculation remains to be tuned. Regular negotiation rules below apply after the introductory sale. **Built 2026-10-22:** the fair offer is the sword's full value and a decline gets no counter-offer; on the second sale the offer card says "Decline and they may offer more" and the first decline always brings a better offer.

1. NPC inspects and offers a price (roughly 80–110% of value, nudged by personality). **Since 2026-10-24 ([[Sessions/2026-10-24#Refinement 4]]) never below value** (Allen: "Customers should never make lowball offers"; he chose "Value to +20%"): Farmer 100–110%, Adventurer 100–115%, Knight 105–120%, ×1.25 when they like the sword. **Since 2026-10-30 ([[Sessions/2026-10-30#Two kinds of customer; the mail popup times out]]) two kinds:** regulars offer 115–150% (Allen: "regular range from 1.15 to 1.50") and VIPs 200–350% ("VIP from 2 - 3.5x"); no favourite swords. A decline gets a raise at 50%, then 25%, 12.5% … ("half it every time"), otherwise the customer leaves; VIPs don't haggle. **The first VIP (2026-11-07):** once a save, the camera follows the first VIP after the tutorial in through the door, they stop and look round (standing still, only the head turns), and once the camera is back on you a banner says "A VIP walked into the store! VIPs offer more money than normal customers!" ([[Sessions/2026-11-07#First VIP cutscene]]). Since 2026-11-08 it waits for a free moment (off stations, windows and pickers, on the ground; the VIP waits by the door up to 22 s, else comes in normally and the next VIP gets it), with letterbox bars, the HUD away and anyone in the way faded ([[Sessions/2026-11-08#First VIP cutscene waits for a free moment]]). **Every VIP is a named character (2026-11-07):** any of the 12 (the nine clients come as VIPs too, as well as clients), with the VIP's offers and their own lines ([[Sessions/2026-11-07#VIPs are the named characters; the first VIP only turns their head]]). They wear their own models' faces (2026-11-09, [[Sessions/2026-11-09#Letter writers' own faces; the new Tanjiro]]).
2. Player chooses **Accept** or **Decline** (since 2026-11-08 the button reads **More [50%]**: the chance declining brings a better offer; "Decline" where they never raise). The tutorial's haggle lesson adds a two-line hint: "Tip: decline for a chance they'll offer more" (2026-11-11).
3. On decline, NPC either:
   - raises the offer (chance based on personality; Pirate rarely, Noble often), or
   - leaves unhappy. Each NPC has a hidden **patience** of 1–3 declines.
4. Accepting fills a small **reputation** meter; reputation raises customer frequency and unlocks special customers. **Built 2026-10-22:** +1 per shop sale, +3 per board order, +5 per commission; levels 1–8 (0/5/15/30/50/80/120/175 points), each shortening walk-ins 6% on top of Better Advertising until 2026-10-27 (Economy pass 2's customer rate: it swamped Better Advertising, so it no longer does); level 3 opens a third board order. It never drops. Special customers are not built ([[Sessions/2026-10-22#Core loop: requests, reputation, heat/luck, HUD, reward card]]). **Since 2026-10-24 the HUD has no star/level pill** (Allen chose "Delivery window only"): the reputation shows as "Rank N" only in the Deliveries window.

**Recorded build:** per-plot displays and customer routes, up to two customers at once (more with the More Clients gem upgrade, and one more with the Shop Promoter since 2026-10-24, two from his Lv. 3 and three at Lv. 5 since 2026-11-07, §10.4; at most 8 with the three Rickshaw Bays) (a walk-in due while the shop is full comes as soon as one leaves, since 2026-11-10), and an offer shown since 2026-10-22 as a bubble over the customer, e.g. "$120 [+20%]" (green above the sword's worth, red below). **Since 2026-10-23 an offer lasts 45 s** (was 20; a starting value to tune, [[Archive/Design/Workshop Urgency Recommendations]]): it rings once, a coin with the seconds left shows over a customer you're too far from (no price), and a waiting customer's card comes 1.5 s after the last one closed. **Since 2026-10-24 every offer has its own card over its customer, several at once,** and a customer says the offer only when its card shows; only the tutorial's first two sales still wait their turn ([[Sessions/2026-10-24#Refinement 4]]). Displayed swords carry no price tag. Before the first sale a listing brings a customer immediately; later arrivals use 12–22 s intervals. The first two ordinary sales use the tutorial exceptions above. **Since 2026-10-31** the first sale's customer never leaves unanswered: a card left alone comes back with a fresh 45 s; since 2026-11-11 that card has no Decline (Accept only, [[Sessions/2026-11-11#Tutorial arrows on the floor, no Decline on the first sale]]). Older NPC opener/preference tables are historical tuning; confirm the live `Shared.Customers` catalogue before balancing. Familiarity remains unimplemented (reputation is built, step 4).

**Cashier upgrade:** an NPC cashier auto-accepts offers above a threshold the player sets (e.g. "accept ≥ 100% of value"). **Built 2026-10-24 as the Cashier employee (§10.4)** (Allen: "if they're offering +20% and my setting is set to anything >10%, then it should be sold. The slider is on his crew page, −20% to +50% in 5% steps, default +10%. Your first two sales stay yours (the tutorial). **Since 2026-10-24 he holds the cash** (Allen: "The cashier should hold all the cash so the player can walk up to him and collect it"; then "press e with him to get the cash out. not just automatically"): he holds a coin pouch with the amount over it, and E at him collects it; a save counts it in your coins.

### 11.1 Settings and comfort
