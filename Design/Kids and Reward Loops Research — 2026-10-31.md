# Kids and Reward Loops Research — 2026-10-31

Allen ([[Sessions/2026-10-31#Refinements, Allen's new notes and the big review (evening)]]): "Do research on how a kid plays a game like roblox, dopamine hits when playing games, and how we can use this research to improve our game."

**How this was made.** Web research on 2026-10-31 (Roblox's filings, Creator Hub and newsroom; peer-reviewed and institutional research; regulators), two lookups in the LihFolk vault (psychology, retention, onboarding and ethics notes), the roblox-player-psychology, roblox-game-design and roblox-growth-design skills, and the vault's design notes and [[Design/Decisions Log]]. **Nothing here is built or played; every change is a proposal for Allen.** The research is general. The Sell Swords! numbers are worked out from its odds and rules, not measured.

**Evidence labels:** **Strong** (replicated experiments or large datasets) · **Mixed** (contested, correlational or small samples) · **Official** (Roblox's own data and rules, regulators) · **Rule of thumb** (practitioner advice: LihFolk vault, skills) · **Maths** (worked out from our odds).

## Summary: the top 10 changes

1. **Beginner's luck with a floor you can see.** After the tutorial, ten ticks on the lever fill one per pull; if no Rare or better has landed by the tenth, the tenth is one. Today about 1 in 5 players get no Rare+ in their first 10 single-slot pulls. Small. Answers Allen's cut-off note "Early game rolls rarity needs a boost"; touches "merging is the only luck" (Refinements 5).
2. **A progress bar on the next sword's pad.** A thin bar under the price fills with your coins and glows when you can buy; buying raises the next pad at once, already part-filled by your change. Small. Stays off the HUD, so it keeps "no pinned goal for now" (2026-10-29).
3. **Book milestones per rarity.** A gift at half and at full for each rarity section of the ingot book, and a plaque in your shop for each full one. Medium. Fits the GDD's planned gems from "Material Index milestones" (§10.1).
4. **Welcome-back icons in the daily window.** What happened while you were away (cart back, letters waiting), today's gift and tomorrow's, as icons and numbers. Small. Respects "Minimal UI text".
5. **The first VIP comes early, once.** One of the first few walk-ins after the tutorial is a VIP for sure, then the normal chance. Today about 60% of players meet no VIP in their first 10 walk-ins. Small. Keeps the Urgency guardrails.
6. **Surprise gifts, never promised.** Now and then a gem tip from a customer or a fan letter with an ingot. Small to medium. Fits the GDD's planned "special customer tips" (§10.1).
7. **Rare moments other players can see.** Legendary and Secret light columns visible over roofs and from the street, with a soft chime; a not-for-sale showcase stand at your shop door. Small (column), medium (stand).
8. **Juice on the strike and the count.** Strikes rise in pitch through the 10 and the last one hits heavier; coin count-ups scale with the amount; full shows only for firsts. Small.
9. **The daily window shows the whole week.** Seven tiles, day 7 bigger with a special gift, tomorrow's gift visible, never a warning about losing anything. Small. Keeps "never resets" (2026-10-22).
10. **Rank metals found, not time played.** Swap the plot leaderboard's playtime board for metals found. Small. Contradicts List.md (2026-10-29).

Also: soften two Robux nudges for a kids' audience, the covering-pack popup on a failed buy and the Starter Pack dot every session ([[#Don'ts]]).

### 1. How kids play Roblox

- **On phones, in short sessions, for a long total.** Four in five DAUs played on mobile in 2024 (Official), and children averaged over an hour a day in the app ([Qustodio](https://www.qustodio.com/en/research/annual-report/), Mixed). Platform-wide that's about 2.7 hours per DAU a day across many games ([Roblox FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509826000024/rblx-20251231.htm), Official); a single game gets much less: LihFolk's casual archetype plays 0.8 sessions a day of about 12 minutes, and phones start throttling around 15 minutes (Rule of thumb). **For us:** phone first (already the rule), and a 5–10 minute visit must finish something.
- **They find games through friends and short clips.** Roblox says discovery runs through the social graph (friends, invites, influencers), Recommended for You (now tuned with age-group data), search and Moments, a short-video feed that links to gameplay; about 40% of the weekly top 100 by impressions were under three months old ([Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001315098/000162828026068235/ex991-q32026earningsshar.htm), Official). Children name friends and YouTubers as what shapes their play and spending ([Children's Commissioner 2019](https://assets.childrenscommissioner.gov.uk/wpuploads/2019/10/CCO-Gaming-the-System-2019.pdf)). A custom 16:9 thumbnail is needed for Home ([[Design/LihFolk Vault Review — 2026-10-30|LihFolk review]]). **For us:** moments worth filming are our advertising.
- **The first minute decides.** Practitioner targets: in control about 10 s after joining, the first real action within 10–20 s, the first reward under 60 s, the whole tutorial under about 3 minutes, objective text of 8 words or fewer, no pop-ups at spawn (LihFolk: First-Time User Experience and Onboarding; Rule of thumb). Roblox: players decide within minutes; show short, mid and long goals; end onboarding on a designed moment of joy ([Roblox: Onboarding](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding), Official).
- **Many can't or won't read.** 40% of US fourth graders read below Basic ([NAEP 2024](https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/reports/reading/2024/g4_8/), Strong); younger children also scroll and navigate less reliably, and practice predicts skill ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/childrens-websites-usability-issues/), Mixed: older studies). Roblox's growth is international, so many players read English as a second language. LihFolk's bar is "comprehensible without text". **For us:** Allen's Minimal UI text rule is right for this audience; teach with ghosts and motion.
- **They learn by watching and copying.** Children copy what they see others do ([Bandura, Ross & Ross 1961](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0045925), Strong for imitation; applying it to games is inference). With Moments and influencers, many kids first see a game played by someone else. **For us:** big moments on a neighbour's plot teach the chase better than a sentence; the tutorial's ghost cursor (built) is the same idea.
- **Friends and status weigh more each year.** In the Children's Commissioner's groups (10–16), skins worked as status; one 10-year-old worried about looking like "trash" in the default skin; many spent to keep up with friends or to progress, some under pressure from friends or strangers (qualitative). With peers watching, teenagers' reward areas respond more and they take more risks; adults don't ([Chein et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3075496), Strong lab). Reward responses in the nucleus accumbens peak in adolescence ([Braams et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4764-14.2015), Strong).
- **They spend to fit in and to get ahead, and often can't tell what it costs.** In a University of Sydney study of 22 children aged 7–14 given a $20 card, most valued what they bought but struggled with Robux and in-game currency conversions and with random rewards; some called them scams or child gambling, and parents felt powerless ([Sydney 2025](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2025/03/24/literally-just-child-gambling-study-urges-swift-regulation-of-roblox.html), Mixed: small qualitative). Parents can set **monthly spending limits** from a linked parent account; they don't cover gift-card redemptions and may not apply on some consoles ([Roblox Help](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/articles/4409125091348), [Roblox newsroom, Nov 2025](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/11/how-to-set-up-spending-limits-and-notifications-on-roblox), Official).
- **Who can see Sell Swords! at all.** Under-16 accounts only see games that passed the Kids or Select evaluation: first a trial visible only to age-checked players 16+, until 250 unique plays from highly engaged age-checked 16+ players within 60 days, plus a safety review; the creator account needs an age check, ID verification, 2-step and either Plus or Premium held 2+ months or a refundable per-game fee ([Creator Hub: Kids and Select](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/kids-and-select), Official). Chat is age-banded worldwide since January 2026 ([Roblox](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/01/roblox-age-checks-required-to-chat), Official). **For us:** the first stats will come from players 16+, not the 9–16 audience in the GDD; don't tune for kids from launch numbers alone. Nothing in the game may need chat.

### 2. How reward loops work

- **Dopamine is about prediction and "wanting", not pleasure.** Dopamine neurons fire for an unexpected reward; once a cue predicts it, the firing moves to the cue, and it dips when an expected reward doesn't come ([Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593), Strong). Dopamine drives "wanting" (the pull of a cue), separately from "liking" (the pleasure itself); addiction is wanting that outgrows liking ([Berridge & Robinson 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0165-0173(98)00019-8), Strong). **For us:** the build-up is part of the reward (the lever, the cascade); a reward that always arrives the same way stops registering; and the payoff has to be genuinely likable (a sword you made, a customer's delight), or we're only selling the craving.
- **Variable rewards hold attention longest, and they're the gambling mechanism.** Variable-ratio schedules give the most persistent responding (Ferster & Skinner 1957, in [[Archive/Design/Urgency Review]]). In loot-box openings, rarer rewards bring more arousal and a stronger urge to open more, and arousal climbs before the reveal ([Larche et al. 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7882574/), Strong lab). LihFolk: put randomness on a dependable base (every sale pays) and keep anything paid transparent (Rule of thumb).
- **Near-misses push people on while feeling worse.** Compared with clear misses, near-misses were less pleasant but raised the wish to keep playing and lit up win-related circuits, in people who weren't gamblers ([Clark et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737/), Strong). In Candy Crush, near-misses were the most frustrating outcome and brought the strongest urge to continue ([Larche, Musielak & Dixon 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5445157/), Strong lab). **For us:** Allen's "no near-miss show" rulings are on the right side.
- **Reveals carry the reward, scaled by rarity.** LihFolk's ladder: Common 0.3–0.5 s up to 5–6 s for the top tiers, each tier adding layers, not just length; first-of-a-kind gets the full show and repeats are compressed; a tap skips; all reward animation under about 10% of play time (Reward Moments and Celebrations; Rule of thumb).
- **Collections pull hardest near the end, and end slowly.** People work harder as a reward gets closer (the goal gradient), and a head start raises completion: 34% against 19% for a car-wash card with two free stamps ([Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf); [Nunes & Drèze 2006](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480), Strong field studies). The last few items of a set take most of the draws, so reward at 50%, 75% and 100%, not only at the end; the next unseen tier should be a median of 1–2 sessions away (LihFolk: Rarity Ladders and Drop Pacing Math; Maths and Rule of thumb). Roblox suggests surfacing collections and quests as visible goals ([Onboarding](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/onboarding), Official).
- **Progress has to be seen, and it dips after a goal.** The same goal-gradient study found effort drops right after a reward is earned, then picks up toward the next ([Kivetz et al. 2006](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf)). **For us:** show the next goal the moment one is reached.
- **Many small wins, a few peaks, a good ending.** Seeing steady daily progress was the strongest everyday motivator in a diary study of workers ([Amabile & Kramer 2011](https://hbr.org/2011/05/the-power-of-small-wins), Mixed for games: workplace evidence). People judge an experience by its peak and its end more than its length ([Kahneman et al. 1993](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x), Strong). Pottery's 3:37 gap after its tutorial sale was the slump Allen felt ([[Design/Pottery Playthrough 2026-10-27]]).
- **Streaks hold people until they break.** An intact streak makes people repeat a behaviour; a broken one makes many drop off, less so if it can be repaired ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029), Strong). Rewards for coming back daily, shown at the moment a child decides whether to stop, are one of the manipulative "lures" in children's apps ([Radesky et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206186/), Strong descriptive). LihFolk: a grace day or a ladder that never resets, never a paid restore, the whole week shown, day 7 different (Rule of thumb). **For us:** our never-resetting ladder is the kid-safe shape.
- **Other people make rewards bigger.** Competence, autonomy and relatedness each independently predicted enjoyment and future play in four studies ([Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8); [Przybylski, Rigby & Ryan 2010](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1037/a0019440), Strong). Peers raise the felt value of rewards in teens ([Chein et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3075496)).
- **Surprise gifts beat promised payments, especially for children.** Preschoolers promised an award for drawing later drew less on their own than children who got the same award unexpectedly or none ([Lepper, Greene & Nisbett 1973](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0035519), Strong classic). A meta-analysis of 128 studies found expected tangible rewards undermined interest while unexpected ones didn't and praise helped; tangible rewards were more harmful, and praise less helpful, for children than for students ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627), Mixed: contested). **For us:** too many "do X, get Y" deals can turn the craft into work; gifts and praise keep it play.
- **Flow: challenge matched to the player.** Jenova Chen's flOw let players set their own difficulty to stay in flow ([Chen 2007](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1232743.1232769)). In an untimed craft, challenge is mostly choice and pacing: short waits, near goals, real decisions (which pad, which offer, which letter).
- **Juice helps, up to a point.** Visual embellishments made games look better in two lab studies but raised felt competence only in some conditions ([Hicks et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1145/3311350.3347171), Mixed: small samples); vibration "juice" improved enjoyment and immersion ([Singhal & Schneider 2021](https://uwaterloo.ca/haptic-experience-lab/projects/juicy-haptic-design-vibrotactile-embellishments-can-improve)). Too much turns into noise: a game drowned in effects had long first sessions and players who didn't return (roblox-game-design field data; Rule of thumb). Keep full-screen flashes to 3 a second or fewer (WCAG 2.3.1, via LihFolk Comfort Toggles). **For us:** juice in proportion to repetition; spectacle saved for rare and first-time moments.
- **The first 10 minutes.** By minute 5: three to five loop completions, a number going up, one real choice, one buy that changes what you do, a priced goal in view, a moment of joy and a next goal; in a tycoon, the first upgrade bought from income by 5 minutes (LihFolk: Onboarding by Genre; Rule of thumb).

### 3. Ethics and limits for a kids' audience

- **What manipulative design looks like in kids' games.** In apps played by 160 children aged 3–5, researchers coded four kinds: *parasocial pressure* (a character guilt-trips or scolds the child into playing on or buying), *fabricated time pressure*, *navigation constraints* (hidden exits, auto-advance) and *lures* (rewards shown just as the child decides whether to stop or buy). 95% of the children's top apps had a feature to prolong play and 80% one to push purchases, more so for lower-income families ([Radesky et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206186/), Strong descriptive).
- **What researchers found in Roblox games.** In 15 top-earning Roblox experiences (October 2025 to February 2026), the University of Sydney found misleading or unfair monetisation in 14: fake discounts and inflated "worth", hidden real cost, unclear odds, near-miss visuals, prompts to spend after a loss, false urgency, upgrades that look required to progress, and unclear effects of not paying ([Sydney 2026](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2026/05/17/inside-the-pay-to-play-world-of-roblox-targeting-children-.html); [report record](https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/handle/2123/35033), Mixed: qualitative).
- **The LihFolk vault's list** adds confirm-shaming, pop-up ambushes on join, pain-to-pay, bait bundles, paid streak restore, public spender status, accidental buys, hidden auto-spend and gifting pressure, and a test: could you explain the feature, and why it exists, to a nine-year-old's parent? (Ethical Design and Player Trust; Rule of thumb)
- **Roblox's rules.** Paid random items: anything random bought directly or indirectly with Robux, including luck boosts and pity systems, must show every outcome's odds in percentages adding to 100 before purchase, behind a labelled button if long (a bare icon isn't enough); a boost's effect is explained before purchase and the odds update while it's active; players flagged `ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted` need another path ([Paid random items](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items), Official). Simulated and real gambling are banned, and so is misleading content used to drive engagement ([Rules of Conduct](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/203313410-Roblox-Rules-of-Conduct), Official). Notifications reach only opted-in players 13+ and must not invent time pressure ([Experience notifications](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/experience-notifications), Official). I found no single Roblox policy named for manipulative design; these are the nearest.
- **Regulators.** UK (OFT principles, 2014, enforced by the CMA): no direct urging of children to buy or to ask others to buy for them, no aggressive practices ([CMA: children's online games](https://www.gov.uk/cma-cases/children-s-online-games), Official). EU consumer authorities (March 2025): show real-money prices, don't hide costs, no pressure tactics or exploiting biases, design around children's vulnerabilities; used against Star Stable's time-limited offers ([Norwegian Consumer Authority](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/eng-articles/national-consumer-authorities-and-commission-take-action-on-harmful-practices-in-the-gaming-industry), Official, soft law). UK ICO Children's Code: no nudges that keep children using a service longer than they meant to; its 2023 tips for games suggest nudging breaks ([ICO code](https://ico.org.uk/media/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services-2-1.pdf); [ICO games guidance](https://cy.ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2023/02/new-guidance-to-industry-issued-for-game-developers-on-protecting-children), Official). US: Epic paid $245 M in refunds over design tricks that caused unwanted purchases ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/80648), Official; see also the FTC's [dark patterns report](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light)). EU proposals: the KIDS Act (17 September 2026) would keep minors away from loot boxes and "variable reward systems", though how it covers games is unclear; the Digital Fairness Act is expected late 2026 ([DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/09/eu-kids-act-online-child-safety-proposal); [European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-protecting-our-democracy-upholding-our-values/file-digital-fairness-act)). **For us:** Lucky Ore and bought Rain are paid luck; keep the restricted path (the found-metal ingot) ready in case rules widen.
- **What the evidence says about harm.**
  - Loot-box spending goes with problem gambling, moderately to strongly among 16–18-year-olds (1,155 surveyed), but the direction is unknown ([Zendle, Meyer & Over 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6599795)); reviews agree on a link, mostly from adult samples ([Spicer et al. 2022](https://pureportal.bcu.ac.uk/en/publications/loot-boxes-problem-gambling-and-problem-video-gaming-a-systematic/)) (Mixed: correlational).
  - Near-misses and rarity raise the urge to go on, in the lab ([Clark 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737/); [Larche 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5445157/), [2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7882574/)) (Strong, short-term).
  - Promised rewards can undermine children's own interest ([Lepper 1973](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0035519); [Deci 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627)) (Mixed).
  - Broken streaks push people away ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029)) (Strong).
  - Children misjudge what virtual currency is worth and feel misled ([Sydney 2025](https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2025/03/24/literally-just-child-gambling-study-urges-swift-regulation-of-roblox.html); [Children's Commissioner 2019](https://assets.childrenscommissioner.gov.uk/wpuploads/2019/10/CCO-Gaming-the-System-2019.pdf)), and some described spending more to chase what they hadn't got (qualitative).
  - Children's sense of chance is weak: fourth to sixth graders failed probability questions, and "3 in 10" style counts helped some sixth graders, though a 2017 Italian replication found little help ([Zhu & Gigerenzer 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2004.12.003)) (Mixed).
- **The line for Sell Swords!** Use these mechanics to make earning, making and showing feel good, never to make stopping or not paying feel bad. Two tests: would it still be fun if nothing were for sale (roblox-game-design), and would a nine-year-old's parent accept the reason for it (LihFolk).

## Applying it to Sell Swords!

### Already in line with the research

- **Rolls:** free and unlimited; the cascade climbs a tier at a time and escalates with rarity (sparks from Rare, shake from Epic, a light column for Legendary and Secret), with **no near-miss show** ([[Design/Decisions Log#Cascading ore roll — 2026-10-28]]; 2026-10-29). NEW on a metal's first roll; dark silhouettes in the book make the missing set visible.
- **Paid luck:** Lucky Ore's odds panel before purchase, and the found-metal ingot where paid random items are restricted ([[Design/Monetization Plan#4. Luck and Roblox's paid random items rules]]).
- **Daily ladder** that never resets ([[Design/Decisions Log]], 2026-10-22 "Gems, daily streak and the Gem Shop").
- **Offers:** none in the tutorial; only where the player already is ([[Design/Monetization Plan#7. How every product works (the monetization skill's rules)|Monetization Plan §7]]); the [[Archive/Design/Urgency Review#5. Guardrails|Urgency guardrails]].
- **Customers:** every offer at or above value (regulars 1.15–1.5×), VIPs glowing gold at 2–3.5×: variable rewards on a floor. Customers say a line about the sword ([[Design/Decisions Log#Shop tension pass — 2026-10-27]]) and wear friends' avatars (2026-10-22).
- **Weather** always on, the better ones rarer (variable interval) ([[Design/Decisions Log#Fixes2 — 2026-10-28]]).
- **Juice:** bought upgrades assemble and burst with a chime; the hilt seat hits by rarity with haptics and a reveal; polish and etch count the value up; coins fly to the counter; four cue sounds, each with a visual twin (2026-10-23, 2026-10-30).
- **Purpose:** letters from named senders; "<sender> wants this!" on a needed ore (2026-10-31).
- **Low load:** only the 3 cheapest pads plus the next sword and the Delivery Cart stand on the floor (2026-10-30); ghost cursors instead of words (2026-10-28).
- **No grind walls on purpose:** the sim counts waits over 15 minutes (2026-10-30).

### Gaps the research points at

- Bad luck has no floor, and Allen noticed early rolls feel thin.
- Progress toward the next goal isn't visible; the sim found casual players drift without a pinned goal (2026-10-29).
- The book is a set with no payoff until you finish it.
- Every surprise is random (rolls, VIPs, weather); nothing arrives as a gift.
- Other players' big moments are hard to see, and there's no way to show a sword off.
- The plot leaderboard ranks time played.
- Two Robux nudges sit at sensitive moments: the covering pack on a failed buy and the Starter Pack dot every session.

### What to measure

- The onboarding funnel ([[Development/Analytics Funnels]]): which step loses the most players, and `OnboardingTime` from the first pull to the first sale.
- New: minutes to the first Rare+ pull, to the first VIP served, the book's % at session end, and whether sessions end right after a goal was reached (the post-reward dip).
- Day-1 return split by whether the first session had a Rare+ and a VIP.
- Counter-metrics: new players' sessions far over 60 minutes (not "longer than intended"), unprompted offers shown per session, and "not enough coins" hits per session.

### Watch

- **Auto Anvil (2026-10-31)** automates the core craft. Making it yourself is part of the value (the IKEA effect; competence): watch whether buyers' sessions get shorter or emptier.
- **Tutorial length.** The lessons now run to the gem buy, longer than LihFolk's ~3-minute rule of thumb; watch funnel steps 9–11 (the letter) for drop-off.
- **Weather always on.** A bonus that's always there becomes the baseline (prediction error), so the rare Rainbow is the one that feels like a reward; keep it rare.
- **Monetization Plan §7 says no offers during a weather, but since Fixes2 a weather is always on.** The intent (no Robux prompt riding on an event's urgency) should be reworded.
- **45 s offers:** urgency between crafts is fine; if kids say they feel rushed, try 60 s ([[Design/Shop Tension Pass#4. Offer timer: measure, don't change yet|Shop Tension Pass §4]]).

## Don'ts

Each of these is a dark pattern for a kids' audience. Where one exists today, the ruling is named.

| Don't | Why | Where it could creep in | Ruling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Near-miss shows:** a reel stopping just short of a top tier, "so close!" | Near-misses feel worse yet push people on (Clark 2009; Larche 2017); the Sydney study found them in Roblox games | Cascade, Auto-Roll, any future wheel | Keeps "no near-miss show" ([[Design/Decisions Log#Cascading ore roll — 2026-10-28]]; 2026-10-29) |
| **A Robux popup over a failed buy** | Prompts to spend after a setback (Sydney 2026), lures at the moment of decision (Radesky 2022), direct urging of children to buy (OFT/CMA) | "Not enough coins" | **Contradicts** [[Design/Decisions Log#Short of coins or gems: offer the covering pack — 2026-10-28]]. A softer version keeps Allen's idea: the shake and notice stay, and the notice gets a small pack button with neutral wording ("800 coins · 9 R$"), at most once a session, never in a player's first sessions. The LihFolk review also asked for a cooldown and a cap. |
| **Fake urgency on anything paid:** countdowns, "limited", "last chance", "Sunshine is ending, buy more!" | Fabricated time pressure (Radesky 2022); false urgency (Sydney 2026); Roblox bans invented time pressure in notifications; EU authorities acted on Star Stable's time-limited offers | Starter Pack, world purchase spots, bought weather, packs | Fits Monetization Plan §7 and the Urgency guardrail (no Robux prompt during an event) |
| **A free taste that expires so the paid one feels like a loss** | Loss-aversion pressure; the psychology skill's "taste of premium" step uses exactly this | First session, weather | — (the free weather rotation is fine; don't frame its end as a loss) |
| **Badging the same paid offer every session** | A lure at decision points (Radesky 2022); at most one unprompted offer in a non-payer's first session (LihFolk) | The Starter Pack's amber dot | **Touches** [[Design/Monetization Plan#12. Built (2026-10-27, not played)\|Monetization Plan §2 and §12]] ("each session"). Show it once after the tutorial, then only when the Shop has something new. |
| **Characters who plead, guilt or scold** | Parasocial pressure was the commonest push in kids' apps (Radesky 2022) | Takeda, customer lines, crew, any seller at purchase spots | Fits pillar 3: customers praise; nobody asks a child to buy |
| **Losing a streak, or buying it back** | Broken streaks drive people away (Silverman & Barasch 2023); paid restore is on LihFolk's list | Daily window | Keeps "never resets" (2026-10-22) |
| **Walls built to sell skips** | The Children's Commissioner: children shouldn't be able to progress by paying; Sydney: upgrades that look required; roblox-game-design: manufactured frustration | Pad prices; the long waits after the Zanbatō | Fits GDD §15 ("anyone can earn everything by playing") and the sim's 15-minute wait check |
| **Hidden, stale or partial odds** | Roblox's paid random items rule; children can't judge chance | Lucky Ore, bought Rain, a floor (#1), Rainbow's VIP chance | Fits Monetization Plan §4; add the floor to the panel if built |
| **Fake value or discount badges** ("worth 500 R$", "-50%" with no real old price) | Misleading prices (Sydney 2026); real-money price transparency (EU authorities) | Shop cards, Starter Pack | — |
| **Ranking who paid** | Public spender status (LihFolk); children's status pressure (Children's Commissioner 2019) | Leaderboards, chat | The [VIP] chat tag ([[Design/Decisions Log#Pass features — 2026-10-27]]) is a style item; never a top-spenders board |
| **Rewarding or ranking time played** | Nudges that keep children playing longer than they meant to (ICO Children's Code) | Plot leaderboard; "play 30 min for a gift" | **Contradicts** the playtime board ([[Design/Decisions Log#List.md - 2026-10-29]]): change #10 |
| **Making leaving hard** ("Are you sure? You'll lose…") | Navigation constraints (Radesky 2022); the ICO suggests natural breaks | Menu, session end | — |
| **Purchase spots that beckon** (pulsing, sounds, a seller NPC) | Lures and parasocial pressure (Radesky 2022) | World purchase spots (built 2026-10-31) | Fits [[Design/Refinements Proposals — 2026-10-30#2. Buying with Robux in the world\|Refinements Proposals §2]] (labelled, not in the tutorial): keep them still and labelled |
| **Offers tied to someone else's rare moment** ("Get Lucky Ore!" after a neighbour's Legendary) | Pushes "wanting" at an emotional peak | Light column (#7) | — |
| **Progress that needs invites, chat or personal details; "Free Robux" framing** | Kids accounts have chat off; Roblox's safety rules; LihFolk's child-safety and marketing-to-kids rules | Friends boost (#14), codes, a future Discord | — |
