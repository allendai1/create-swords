# Sword Profile Ideas

More blade shapes to pick from, after the tantō, wakizashi and katana. Already listed elsewhere and not repeated here: the ten sheet profiles in [[Art/Sword Silhouettes|Sword Silhouettes]] (dagger, shortsword, longsword, katana, rapier, cutlass, saber, scimitar, claymore, flamberge).

**Fit** says how the shape suits the current blade model ([[Development/Blade Profile Authoring#How easy it is to add a sword|Blade Profile Authoring]]):

- **Easy**: a single edge with an outline that is a simple curve along the length. Data only.
- **Care**: double edges, wavy edges, very strong curves or sharp steps. Needs workarounds or more stations.
- **New feature**: hooks, forks, holes, serrations or barbs. Needs new mesh work.

Lengths are blade only (nagasa), without the tang.

## Japanese

| Sword | Silhouette that sets it apart | Length · curve | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tachi** | Deep curve that peaks near the hilt (koshi-zori) and narrows a lot toward a small point. Reads "older and more elegant" than the katana. | ~75 cm · ~2.8 cm | Easy |
| **Nodachi / Ōdachi** | A katana stretched past 90 cm, wide and heavy. Big two-handed silhouette. | 90–150 cm · ~3 cm | Easy |
| **Ō-kissaki katana** | Wide blade that barely narrows, ending in an oversized point (6 cm+). Aggressive, easy to tell from the katana. | ~72 cm · ~1.5 cm | Easy |
| **Naginata-naoshi** | A polearm blade reforged into a sword: narrow near the hilt, flaring wide in the top third, with the curve concentrated near the point. | ~55 cm · ~1.5 cm | Easy |
| **Shōbu-zukuri** | Iris-leaf blade with no yokote: the ridge line runs straight into the point, so the tip is a long, sharp taper. | ~50 cm · ~1.2 cm | Easy |
| **Unokubi-zukuri** | "Cormorant neck": the back is thinned in the upper half, so the blade looks slimmer near the tip from the side. | ~68 cm · ~1.8 cm | Easy (thickness curve) |
| **Chokutō** | The ancient straight sword: flat sides, no curve, single edge, small angled point. Plain but very readable. | ~65 cm · 0 | Easy |
| **Yoroi-dōshi** | Armour-piercing tantō: short, very thick at the base, triangular section. Stubby and brutal. | ~22 cm · 0 or slight inward | Easy |
| **Kogarasu-maru** | Curved blade whose top half is double-edged (kissaki-moroha-zukuri). A real one-of-a-kind; good special design. | ~63 cm · ~1.2 cm | Care (back becomes an edge only near the tip) |
| **Tsurugi / Ken** | Ancient straight double-edged sword with a central ridge and a symmetrical point. | ~60 cm · 0 | Care (double-edged) |
| **Ninjatō** | Pop-culture straight short sword with a squared, chisel-like point. Not historical; label it as a fun one. | ~55 cm · 0 | Easy |

## Western

| Sword | Silhouette that sets it apart | Length · curve | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gladius** | Short and broad. The Mainz type is slightly waisted with a long point; the Pompeii type is parallel with a short triangular point. | ~50 cm · 0 | Care (double-edged) |
| **Viking sword** | Wide, flat, gently tapering, with a rounded, almost spoon-like tip. | ~78 cm · 0 | Care (double-edged) |
| **Falchion** | Single edge that **widens toward the point**, like a cleaver, then clips up to the tip. The most distinct Western outline here. | ~70 cm · slight | Easy |
| **Messer** | German "knife-sword": single edge, slight curve, a clipped back near the tip. A Western cousin of the katana. | ~75 cm · ~1 cm | Easy |
| **Broken-back seax** | Straight edge; the back runs straight, then breaks at an angle down to the point. | 30–75 cm · 0 | Easy (sharp spine point) |
| **Falcata** | Forward-bent recurve: the edge is hollow near the hilt and swells into a belly before the point. | ~50 cm · recurved | Care (two bends) |
| **Zweihänder** | Huge blade with a long blunt ricasso and parrying hooks just above the guard. | ~130 cm · 0 | Care; hooks are New feature (or put them in the hilt) |
| **Estoc** | Long, stiff, edgeless thrusting spike with a diamond section. Very thin from the front. | ~100 cm · 0 | Care |
| **Smallsword** | Short, slender, triangular-section court sword. An elegant late unlock. | ~78 cm · 0 | Care |
| **Shotel** | Ethiopian sickle sword: an almost half-circle curve. Very striking on a rack. | ~85 cm (along the curve) · extreme | Care (the bend may need more stations) |
| **Khopesh** | Egyptian sickle sword: straight shaft, then a hooked blade with the edge on the outside. | ~55 cm · hooked | New feature |

## Other traditions (if a third tradition comes)

| Sword | Silhouette | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Jian** (China) | Straight, double-edged, narrow, with a sharp but rounded point. | Care |
| **Dao / Liuyedao** (China) | Single edge, gentle curve, a willow-leaf widening near the tip. | Easy |
| **Kukri** (Nepal) | Short, bent forward at the middle, heavy belly. | Care (strong bend) |
| **Yatagan** (Ottoman) | Edge on the inside of a forward recurve, like a stretched falcata. | Care |
| **Kris** (Indonesia) | Wavy blade, often asymmetric at the base. | Care (more stations, like the flamberge) |
| **Kampilan** (Philippines) | Long, narrow at the hilt, widening to a forked tip. | New feature (fork) |

## Fictional

| Sword | Silhouette | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Zanbatō** | A cleaver-like slab as tall as the player, nearly the same width from base to a squared tip. Great late-game showpiece. | Easy |
| **Sakabatō** | A katana with the edge on the **inside** of the curve. A one-line twist on the katana; fun commission. | Easy (flip the curve) |
| **Crescent Fang** | The back curves inward like a new moon while the edge bulges out, so the blade is thin at both ends and fat in the middle. | Easy |
| **Shard Blade** | Faceted crystal look: a straight back, an edge made of three flat runs that meet at corners, and a diagonal chisel tip. | Care (sharp corners) |
| **Ember Tongue** | A katana whose edge waves like flames only in its top half, so it flickers on the rack. Pairs with [[Art/Flame Etch|Flame Etch]]. | Care (more stations) |
| **Serpent** | The whole blade snakes in an S: back and edge wave together, like a stretched kris. | Care |
| **Leviathan Cleaver** | Very wide falchion with a notch cut out of the back near the tip. | New feature (notch) if it is a hole; Easy if it is a step |
| **Twin Fang** | A blade that splits into two points at the tip. | New feature (fork) |
| **Sawtooth** | Long serrated teeth along the back, smooth edge in front. | New feature (serrations) |
| **Moonhook** | The tip curls back over itself into a hook. | New feature (hook) |

## Suggested order

Allen 2026-10-23: "I feel like more japanese swords is a bit redundant, they look very similar". So the order now picks swords whose outlines look clearly different at rack distance: a different length class, width profile, curve or point. The order has not been decided.

1. **Japanese line stops at the katana**, plus at most one showpiece that doesn't look like a katana: **Zanbatō** (a giant squared slab). Tachi, nodachi, ō-kissaki, shōbu, unokubi and Sakabatō all read as "a katana" from a few studs away, so they're dropped as blueprints. They could come back later as cosmetic variants, not as new blueprints.
2. **Western tradition, each one a different silhouette:**
   - **Longsword:** the knight's first commission ([[Design/Alignment Review — 2026-10-23|Alignment Review]] question 9). Straight and double-edged.
   - **Falchion:** a cleaver that gets wider toward the point.
   - **Gladius:** short and broad.
   - **Rapier or Estoc:** a needle.
   - **Zweihänder:** huge.
   - **Shotel:** a half circle.

   Skip the messer and Viking sword. They sit too close to the katana and the longsword.
3. **Fictional specials:** **Crescent Fang** (Easy), **Shard Blade** and **Ember Tongue** (Care). Leave the New feature ones until hooks or forks are worth building.

The double-edged swords need the Care workaround once; after that, they can all reuse it.

**Picked 2026-10-23:** Allen chose the **Longsword, Falchion and Gladius**, each with its own hilt, and the hilt is now fitted automatically. The double-edged workaround is built: the profile field `doubleEdged` ([[Development/Blade Profile Authoring#Western blades (2026-10-23)]], [[Sessions/2026-10-23#Western swords and automatic hilts]]).

**Big swords, 2026-10-23:** Allen proposed a big-sword category (two-handed, one per standing rack, high price) and gave three buster-sword references. The **Zanbatō** is the first: profile `zanbato`, see [[Development/Blade Profile Authoring#Big swords (2026-10-23)]]. The Zweihänder, claymore and flamberge are the other big candidates.
