## Etchings that pop (strong glow)

Allen: "Any way to make the etchings pop? Also what color is the etching? Is it always the same? What if the blade is black or dark blue, then wont it be hard to see". Answer: a stroked etch paints a fixed near-black body (6, 12, 20) over the whole face and draws its marks in a vivid version of the metal (`Etches.MATERIAL_FLAME`: silver Iron, gold Gold, blue Blue Steel, purple Obsidian, violet Starmetal…), so the body is always dark and only the marks change colour. In a render the marks were faint on every metal and hard to read on Blue Steel, Obsidian and Starmetal: they dimmed to 65% away from the light, were thin, and blue/purple on near-black has little contrast. Neon on the overlay does nothing (the texture overrides it).

Three looks were rendered side by side (today, glow, strong glow; `References/Atmosphere/etch-pop-compare.jpg`); Allen: "Strong glow".

- **`Shared/EtchCanvas`:** strokes 1.7× wider; the mark's core is the accent lifted 55% toward white; marks dim only 12% away from the light; a soft halo of the accent spreads 0.24 cm around every mark and fill, up to 85% of the way from the body to the accent. Applies to all stroked patterns (Eclipse, Tide, Stormroot, Woven Wind, Fracture, Falling Stars), on held and displayed swords, cards, the hotbar and the etching show. Flame (the band) is unchanged.
- **Cost:** the halo made a build about 6× slower (three katana patterns: 530 ms → 3.0 s in Studio). Painting each pixel once per reveal, skipping pixels beyond the halo without a square root, and stroke pieces of 0.25 cm (were 0.1) brought it to 1.2 s (about 2.3× the old). Swords in the world now build their etch over several frames (`SwordRenderer` passes `spread`); cards and the hotbar still build at once.
- **Trade-off:** the whiter core makes neighbouring colours closer (Copper's marks read pale peach, Blue Steel and Starmetal are nearer each other).
- **Checked in Edit:** `EtchingSpec` 36 checks, 0 failed (progressive reveal still equals the finished image), the suite in 16 s (was 55 s with the first glow); `SwordRenderer` compiles; a render of eight etched katanas with the real code. **Not played.**

## Economy pass 1

Allen took decisions 1–3 of the review as recommended: "one new board order every ~3 minutes, orders pay 1.2× the handed-in sword's value (deliveries 1.3×), and polish multiplies the whole value by 1.10."

- **Board clock (`Shared/Orders`, `ShopService`):** the board still opens full when the first request is handed in. After that, filling an order starts a 3-minute clock (`Orders.EVERY` 180), and an order comes each time it runs out while the board has room. The clock is `os.time` saved as `orderAt`, so the board fills while you're away (one order per 3 minutes missed). A new slot at reputation 3 waits its turn. `ShopService` checks the clock every second per plot. On the board, a dimmed **New order · in m:ss** card (VectorIcons Scroll) shows when the next one comes.
- **Order pay:** an order pays `Orders.pay`, 1.2× the handed-in sword's own value, so metal, hilt, finishes and quality all count: a level-1 crafter's polished katana with the Gem hilt pays 212, a polished and etched common katana with the Gem hilt 468, a Wooden common katana 280 (365 before). The board card says "+20% value · N gems"; each fitting sword in the hand-in picker shows its pay. Orders no longer store a reward, and reputation no longer adds 5% a level (it still opens the third order, Rare orders and the extra gem).
- **Delivery pay:** collecting pays `Deliveries.pay`, 1.3× the packed swords' value. An offer card shows "+30%" until something is packed, then the coins packed so far. A delivery already on its way when this went in pays by the new rule.
- **Polish:** `SmithyCore.swordValue` multiplies the whole value by 1.10 for a fully polished sword (it was +10% added to the hilt, seat and etch bonuses). A common katana with the Gem hilt and an etch is 390 polished (376 before); a Legendary one 4,675 (4,514). The polishing texts ("+10% value") now say exactly what happens.
- **Checked in Edit:** all eight changed scripts compile; `Orders`, `Deliveries` and `SmithyCore` load from fresh clones; `SaveSpec` 66/66 (12 new: seven cases of the clock, order pay, delivery pay, polish ×1.10, `orderAt` saved and missing from older saves). **Not played.**
- **Backups:** `ServerStorage.Legacy.<Name>_beforeEconomyPass` for SmithyCore, ShopService, DeliveryService, Orders, Deliveries, CommissionController, DeliveryController and SaveSpec.
- **Side effect to decide:** the cart pays 1.3× for any sword with only the trip as a limit (2–3 swords per 150 s trip before upgrades; offers refill at once), so once bought it beats the shop's average first offer (about 95% of value) for every sword. Decision 20 in [[Design/Alignment Review — 2026-10-23#Decisions waiting]].

**Allen needs to save the place.** Playtest: hand in the Wakizashi (two orders appear); fill one: you get 1.2× that sword's value and the board shows "New order · in 3:00"; wait 3 minutes, or leave and come back later, and the order is there; fill both and two come back 3 minutes apart. ### Big swords: findings and open risks

What I learned or noticed during the Zanbatō and Big Sword Stand work, for whoever picks up big swords next:

- **Scale:** at 0.05 studs per cm a katana is already about 4.9 studs with its hilt, close to a 5-stud player. A real-scale big sword dwarfs the player, which is why Allen chose 100 cm (6.4 studs) over 130 cm (about 8). Size future big swords in studs next to the katana.
- **Copyright:** the references are Square Enix's Buster Sword. The two holes and the bolted collar make it recognisable, so the Zanbatō leaves them out. Keep its own hilt original too.
- **Long blades and the tests:** the JS suite assumed one edge-to-edge grinding pass nearly finishes a blade. That holds only up to about 90 cm; the setup now grinds until done. Grinding a 98 cm blade takes three passes, and a 128 cm one took four.
- **Plot template:** in Edit, Upgraded Store items overlap the starter workshop. After the push, the shop's back wall is the workshop's door wall (plot z -6.52, a post at x -13, the door at x 0).
- **Studio's `Tests.BladeGolden`** has only the Japanese blades; neither the western nor the Zanbatō export copied it. `BladeCoreSpec` therefore doesn't check those blades against the JS core.
- **Open risks, not checked:**
  - Crew training: `Employees.TRAIN_PRICE` has no `zanbato`. If you craft a Zanbatō, the crew window may offer training and ask `trainPrice("zanbato")`, which may be nil.
