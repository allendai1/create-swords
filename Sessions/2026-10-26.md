## Test Anvil blades keep their shape

Allen: "can you wire in our test anvil so that i can also hilt the blade produced from it". The Test Anvil's blade already reached the Hilt Bench, but as the blueprint's standard shape. Asked, he picked "Keep the shape I made": the hilted, displayed and sold sword keeps the exact shape he hammered.

- **The blade keeps its widths.** Finish sends the widths along with the match (cm per station). `FinishShaped` checks them (one per station, each a number between 0 and 60 cm), rounds them to 0.001 cm and stores them as the blade's `shape`. The `shape` moves with the blade into the finished sword, is saved and loaded with it, and is sent to the client with the carried blades and swords (and the racks' sword lists).
- **One rule for the geometry:** `BladeCore.withShape(blade, params, shape)` puts the shaped width in place of the blueprint's, about the finished back line (double-edged: about the finished centre line), exactly as the shaping prototype draws it. Thickness, bevel and the rest stay the blueprint's. Polishing changes only the finish of a shaped blade; its widths stay yours.
- **Every view uses it:** `SwordMeshCache.blade`, `overlay` and `etch` take an optional `shape`. A shaped blade gets its own mesh keyed by its widths, built live (never baked). Callers pass it on: the Hilt Bench's seating view and blade cards, held and displayed swords (`SwordRenderer`, from a `BladeShape` attribute `SmithyCore.buildSword` sets), hotbar slots, picker cards (shop, racks, etching, polishing, reward, delivery), the Etching Table's show and the Polishing Station's live blade.
- Blades from Anvils 1 and 2 have no `shape` and are unchanged, sharing the baked meshes as before.

## Freeing shaped blades from the EditableMesh budget (research)

Allen: "Look into 2", option 2 of three for the live-mesh limit (convert each finished blade into a regular MeshPart, then free the live mesh).

- **The plain call doesn't do it.** `BladeView` already makes its part with `AssetService:CreateMeshPartAsync(Content.fromObject(mesh))`, but that part stays linked to the live `EditableMesh` (edits show on it at once), so the mesh, and its budget, has to stay alive.
- **`AssetService:CreateDataModelContentAsync` does.** It bakes an EditableMesh (or EditableImage) into static, session-only content; `CreateMeshPartAsync(content)` makes a MeshPart from it, and the EditableMesh can then be destroyed. Roblox: baked content doesn't count against the Editable* memory budgets; it's paged to disk (server-made content capped at 200 MB in all, client-made content by disk space); server-made content replicates to every client; nothing is uploaded or kept after the session. Full release 2026-03-27, live in published experiences since 2026-05-18 ([announcement](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/full-release-introducing-createdatamodelcontent-convert-editable-mesh-and-image-data-into-static-content/4541898)).
- **Tried in Edit** on a shaped katana (the narrow-tip preset, half polished): build 8.6 ms, bake 16.7 ms, MeshPart 18.2 ms. With the EditableMesh destroyed, the baked part read back with the same 785 vertices and 769 triangles, every vertex within 0.000000 studs of the live one, its vertex colours (the polish finish) kept, and the same size; a clone shares the content. Vertex ids come back in a different order, which nothing here relies on.
- **Not measured:** that the budget really frees on a client. Edit and plugins have no budget, so this needs a Play client: build shaped blades until creation fails, then the same with baking.
- **How it would go in:** in `SwordMeshCache`, a mesh built live (shaped blades, and any blueprint without a bake) would be baked and its EditableMesh destroyed before it becomes the template, for blade, overlay and Flame meshes. The live editors (the anvil, the Polishing Station's blade) keep their EditableMeshes. A stroked etch's canvas is an EditableImage on the overlay and could be baked the same way. Each client bakes what it sees; baking once on the server at Finish, so it replicates to everyone, is the bigger alternative.
