## Etch refinement

Allen: "Refine the etchings. the falling stars look off. and fix the issues you pointed out." He pasted a critique of the pack (zanbatō placement, scale and tip distortion; crescent openings and woven crossings; Stormroot's start, Fracture's composition, Falling Stars' trails; a fixed halo that outlines small blades heavily; contrast on Iron, Black Iron, Gold and a busy textured metal at phone size). Built in Studio; not played.

- **Motif scale and placement (`EtchPatterns.strokes`):** generators see the width capped at `EtchCanvas.MOTIF_CM` (7 cm); on a wider face (only the zanbatō, 18.5 cm) their strokes are moved to its middle. Motifs stop at `limitOf`: the first place past 40% of the blade where the width falls faster than 0.25 cm per cm (zanbatō chisel, gladius point, a katana's kissaki), minus half a width. Generators take `(length, widthAt, ctx)` with `ctx.limit` and `ctx.strips` (strips from the real width).
- **Halo by width (`EtchCanvas`):** the soft halo is `HALO` 0.07 of the capped mean width (tantō about 0.16 cm, katana 0.19, zanbatō 0.49; it was a fixed 0.24). A stroke may carry `ink = { solid, fade }` for a crisp border instead. `EtchCanvas.halo(mean)`, `THICK`, `CORE` and `MOTIF_CM` are exported.
- **Eclipse:** crescents thinner (half-width up to 0.06 × width, was 0.107) with a ±55° mouth (was ±42°); the sliver smaller (0.42 r, ±60°) and clear of the crescent's ends; connectors end 0.2 widths from a ring. Tails stop at the limit.

## Anvil shaping: fewer hits, a true-width guide, sanding any time

Allen: "i like the way our anvil prototyping is going. one thing is that it takes too many manual hits to fully forge the shape. i also feel like the guide is not representative of the blade itself? also feel like the accuracy needs to be a bit more generous, and players should be able to smooth whenever." Asked, he picked:

- **Hold to hammer:** "maybe instead of manually hitting they can also hold down to hit automatically?", a steady beat that follows the mouse. Holding the mouse (or the touch Strike button) keeps striking, one strike as each finishes (about 2.8 a second), wherever you aim. A click is still one strike.
