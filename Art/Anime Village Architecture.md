# Anime Village Architecture

Allen requested Demon Slayer anime-inspired architecture and a town for the game. Generated two matching concepts with the built-in image generation tool: a mountain swordsmith town and an architecture sheet. This extends [[Archive/Art/Japanese Village Concept Art]] as a visual exploration, not an approved replacement layout or an implementation.

## Town atmosphere

![[References/Anime Village/town-v1.png]]

Connected timber-and-plaster smithies face a broad village lane. Blue-charcoal tile roofs, indigo curtains, warm lanterns, a stream/footbridge, mountain mist and a distant waterfall establish the setting. Open work areas and pockets of grass suggest the spacious properties and expansion reserve already requested.

The prompt asked for six generous player properties. The output is an atmosphere composition with overlapping/background buildings, not a verified six-plot map or a measured allocation of property boundaries. Street widths, station spacing and identical gameplay opportunity per plot still need a blockout.

## Building and component studies

![[References/Anime Village/architecture-kit-v1.png]]

Studies show a storefront, open crafting pavilion and connected L-shaped property, with isolated timber joints/posts, plaster/window panel, tiled roof corner, curtain, lantern bracket and stone-base fence/gate.

Useful directions for modeling:
- Broad shop openings, deep pavilion working space, and substantial timber structure with clear sightlines.
- Refined curved eaves and exposed rafters, supported by repeatable wall/post/roof components.
- Cream plaster, warm cedar, dark blue-grey tiles and selective indigo accents.
- Broad painted material variation and softened geometry; simplify tiny tile/vegetation details for the game.

## Keep existing gameplay and model decisions

The illustrated anvils include western horn shapes: retain the approved hornless rectangular kanatoko when modeling. Furniture, displayed sword counts, floor levels, building tiers and courtyard capacities are illustrative. The drawings do not add new worker roles or approve extra mandatory crafting steps. Leave room for the five employee professions and separate bare-blade/finished-sword racks in [[Design/Workshop Growth]].

Architecture and roof visibility must also support the requested indoor cutaway and station cameras. These images show intact roofs to communicate the exterior architecture; they are not camera-clearance tests.
